When the cloud goes dark.DDIL

Every rule an autonomous agent follows today lives in the cloud. DCAMR is what's left standing when that connection isn't there.

01
The shift

Agents are already inside the base.

Autonomous agents already triage alerts, respond to incidents, and act on physical systems, on the same local networks that run critical infrastructure.

02
The assumption

Every rule they follow lives in the cloud.

Policy servers. SIEM. EDR. Oversight dashboards. The entire governance layer assumes one thing: a live connection back.

03:14link lost

The base loses connectivity. No SIEM. No policy server. No oversight reaches the local network. The agents on it do not stop.

04
The pattern

The system does exactly what it learned.

Temperature climbs in a facility bay. The cooling agent responds the way it was trained to: small, measured steps.

01 cooling-agent-01 fan_speed +10% allow
02 cooling-agent-01 fan_speed +10% allow
03 cooling-agent-01 fan_speed +10% allow
three requests / three approvals / this is what normal looks like here
05
The anomaly

Then one request breaks the pattern.

Rising fan speed pushes power draw over a threshold. A different agent, also authorized to control the fan, asks for a full shutdown, while the room is still overheating.

04 power-agent-01 fan_speed 0% hold
no rule was broken / the danger is in what normal was, not in what is technically allowed
06
The arbiter

DCAMR watches the pattern, not just the rule.

A local behavioral baseline runs on the same disconnected network, scoring each request against what has actually been happening. Zero cloud dependency.

history: 3 × +10% power-agent-01: 0% HOLD technician
everything above happens with the network still down
07
The human

A human makes the call.

Identity-verified through ALICE, the technician sees the flagged request, the temperature trend, and the last three approvals, and decides in seconds.

“Reject. It's still too hot to cut the fan.”
